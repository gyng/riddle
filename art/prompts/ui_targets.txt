=== WHAT THE GAME IS ===
The player never controls the hero directly. They write an ordered list of rules (up to 8 rows,
each "condition -> action", e.g. "HP < 30% -> drink heal", "foes >= 3 -> retreat to corridor",
"depth >= 8 -> bank") and send the hero into a dungeon; the hero plays by those rules. A live
forecast shows how deep he will likely get. When he dies, a verdict screen names the rule that
failed and offers patch rows. Runs also happen overnight; a report shows what happened.

=== THE STYLE REFERENCE: Warcraft III and StarCraft II interfaces ===
A sprite-based, skeuomorphic, DIEGETIC game interface: the chrome is a physical object in the
game's world. Carved dark stone and hammered iron bezels with rivets and corner caps; a heavy
ornate CONSOLE along the bottom third (like WC3's race console); a round PORTRAIT WELL with a
character face at the console's left, ringed by a health ring; a COMMAND CARD grid of square
ability tiles in the console's middle; a big glowing GEM button at the console's right (the
primary action); a RESOURCE BAR plate across the top with engraved icons and numbers; framed
TOOLTIP panels with an iron frame around aged parchment. Warm torchlight on metal edges, bevels,
inset shadows, worn gilding, subtle runes. Dark fantasy, readable, NOT cluttered: a phone at
arm's length reads about twelve things per screen. Palette: umber stone, blackened iron, bone
and parchment, one accent of amber torchlight; crimson only for danger and death; teal-green for
health. Typography in the mockup: a carved serif for titles (like Cinzel), condensed sans for
numbers; any text must be short and legible (single words and numbers), never paragraphs.
Painterly-but-crisp game UI concept art, like a Blizzard UI concept sheet rendered as a single
phone screenshot. NOT flat web design, NOT material design, NOT a mobile gacha UI, no logos,
no watermarks, no real brand names.
